Early Stage — Mild Impairment
Duration typically 2–4 years
  • Forgetting recent conversations or appointments
  • Difficulty finding the right words
  • Losing or misplacing everyday objects
  • Trouble planning or managing money
  • Mild mood swings / social withdrawal
  • Getting confused in unfamiliar places
Patient can still live independently with some support. This is the best time to plan future care arrangements.
🛡️
Caregiver Actions
What you should do right now
  • Book a neurologist/geriatrician assessment
  • Set up a medication reminder app or pill box
  • Install door alarms and GPS tracker
  • Remove fall hazards — loose rugs, sharp corners
  • Label cabinets, drawers, and common items
  • Discuss legal & financial planning (power of attorney)

Middle Stage — Moderate Impairment
Duration typically 2–10 years (longest stage)
  • Forgetting personal history and family names
  • Wandering — especially at night
  • Increasing confusion about time and place
  • Needs help with dressing, bathing, toileting
  • Paranoia, hallucinations, or delusions
  • Difficulty recognising close family members
24/7 supervision is strongly recommended. Consider professional in-home care or a memory care facility.
🔒
Safety Priorities
Critical actions for this stage
  • Install door & window locks with alarms
  • Use stove auto-shutoff or remove knobs
  • Secure medications in a locked cabinet
  • Put up large-print photos with name labels
  • Get a medical ID bracelet engraved
  • Contact ARDSI / local day-care programs
❤️
Late Stage — Severe Impairment
Full-time care required
  • Loss of ability to speak or communicate
  • Requires help for all daily activities
  • Difficulty swallowing — risk of aspiration
  • Increased vulnerability to infections
  • May become bed-bound
  • Loss of bladder and bowel control
Focus on comfort, dignity, and quality of life. Palliative care and hospice services may be appropriate.
💙
Caregiver Support
You matter too — please seek help
  • Reach out to ARDSI for palliative care resources
  • Use respite care services to take regular breaks
  • Join a caregiver support group (online or local)
  • Speak to a counsellor or psychologist yourself
  • Discuss advance directives and end-of-life wishes
